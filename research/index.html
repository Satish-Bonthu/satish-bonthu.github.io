PhD research

A wave-based simulation can predict how sound travels and decays in a room, but repeated simulations become expensive when you change the wall materials or other inputs. I build reduced-order models to make those repeated predictions faster while preserving the acoustic behavior that matters.

Published research on numerical stability

Our published formulation preserves the structure of the time-domain equations during model reduction and accounts for frequency-dependent absorbing boundaries.

Read the open-access paper

Ongoing research on changing room materials

I’m investigating how a shared reduced basis can predict impulse responses across a range of boundary impedances. Accuracy and stability are assessed against full-order simulations.

See the research project at ACUTE

Non-intrusive modeling and data sparsity

Our 2026 SSRN preprint combines proper orthogonal decomposition with regression to study how coverage of surface-absorption settings affects prediction accuracy.
