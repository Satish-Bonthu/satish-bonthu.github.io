PhD Research

A wave-based simulation can predict how sound travels and decays in a room, but repeated simulations become expensive when you change the wall materials or other inputs. I build reduced-order models to make those repeated predictions faster while preserving the acoustic behavior that matters.

Published research on numerical stability

Problem
Model reduction can introduce unstable pressure growth in time-domain room simulations.
Proposed solution
Preserve the structure of the acoustic equations during reduction.
How it was approached
Tested against full-order simulations with rigid and absorbing boundaries.
Level of difficulty
Advanced · Coupled boundary dynamics and stability after projection.
Value and takeaway
Demonstrates a route to faster, stable predictions in the tested cases.
Sustainability
Lower compute time can reduce computational demand for repeated studies.

Ongoing research on changing room materials

Problem
Changing wall absorption typically requires another full simulation.
Proposed solution
Use a shared reduced basis across a range of wall impedances.
How it was approached
Compare predictions at unseen in-range impedances with full-order responses.
Level of difficulty
Advanced · The basis must capture how decay changes across materials.
Value and takeaway
Could make material comparisons faster during acoustic design.

Work in progress · Unreviewed. Shared to illustrate the research direction. Constructive questions and feedback are welcome.

Non-intrusive modeling and data sparsity

Problem
A surrogate can lose accuracy in poorly sampled material settings.
Proposed solution
Combine proper orthogonal decomposition with regression.
How it was approached
Use full-order room simulations to assess how training coverage affects predictions.
Level of difficulty
Advanced · Sparse regions are hard to diagnose as the number of inputs grows.
Value and takeaway
Shows where targeted training simulations can improve prediction accuracy.

SSRN preprint · Unreviewed manuscript. Shared for research discussion. Constructive feedback is welcome.
