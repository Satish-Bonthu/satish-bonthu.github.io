Satish Bonthu

I’m a mechanical engineer with over 15 years of CFD consulting experience. My PhD research develops reduced-order models for wave-based room acoustics, with the goal of predicting impulse responses at lower computational cost.

ACUTE Research Laboratories · University of Iceland

PhD research

A wave-based simulation can predict how sound travels and decays in a room, but repeated simulations become expensive when you change the wall materials or other inputs. I build reduced-order models to make those repeated predictions faster while preserving the acoustic behavior that matters.

Published research on numerical stability

Our published formulation preserves the structure of the time-domain equations during model reduction and accounts for frequency-dependent absorbing boundaries.

Read the open-access paper

Ongoing research on changing room materials

I’m investigating how a shared reduced basis can predict impulse responses across a range of boundary impedances. Accuracy and stability are assessed against full-order simulations.

See the research project at ACUTE

Lab prototypes

Room Resonance Lab

An interactive prototype for exploring how room geometry shapes natural frequencies and the spatial patterns of acoustic modes.

Exciting a furnished room

A planned standalone prototype comparing how different source signals excite the modes of the same furnished room. The aim is to connect modal analysis to a forced response through examples such as a clap, a whistle, and a balloon pop.

Didgeridoo resonance

An exploration of how the length and shape of a didgeridoo’s bore affect its resonances, including a snake-shaped geometry.

CFD · Building ventilation

Can the wind ventilate a restroom?

I compared simulated suction from a wind-driven ventilator with experimental data and used a passive tracer to explore how effectively it clears air from a naturally ventilated restroom.

Ray optics

Tokarsky’s unilluminable room

A ray-tracing experiment on a geometric puzzle about whether light from one point can reach every part of a mirrored room.
